NextPoint is a score keeper. It records the points you tell it were played and applies the published rules of each sport to say what the score is, who serves and when a game, set or match is over. It is not an official record of any competition.

Agreement

By using NextPoint you accept these terms. NextPoint is also covered by Apple's standard licence agreement for apps, the Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions (EULA), which applies in full.

What NextPoint is for

Keeping your own record of the matches you play: the score point by point, who served, the calls the rules required, and the rivalries that build up over time. The rules NextPoint applies are the published rules of each sport as they stood on the date shown in the app's rules library. Rules change; an umpire on a court, an official at a tournament and the rules of your own club take precedence over anything NextPoint says.

Purchases

NextPoint starts you with a small number of matches. The full app has no count on them, and is sold as a monthly or a yearly subscription, or as a one time purchase, through the App Store. A subscription renews on its own until you cancel it in your Apple Account settings, at least a day before it renews; the one time purchase never renews. Prices are shown in your own currency on the purchase screen before you buy. Refunds are handled by Apple.

Your matches

Everything you score is yours. It stays on your devices and in your own iCloud, and you can export it or delete it at any time. NextPoint claims no rights over it and cannot see it.

No warranty

NextPoint is provided as is. It is built to apply the rules correctly and tested against them, but no software is without mistakes, and a disputed point on a court is settled by the people playing it, not by an app.
